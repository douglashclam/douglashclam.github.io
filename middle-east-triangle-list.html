The Middle East
by the triangle scale

The cradle of civilisation and the Silk Road's western half — extraordinary heritage density rivalling Europe, but shadowed more than any other region by safety and access. Many summits here are rated on merit with a hard feasibility asterisk.

Anatolia & the Levant
Turkey Transcontinental · the bridge
▲▲▲
Istanbul
The only city straddling two continents, with three world-empires layered in one place — Hagia Sophia (the supreme Byzantine monument), the Blue Mosque, Topkapı, the Cistern, the Bosphorus. Rivals Rome and Paris as a heritage city. your Byzantine wavelength
▲▲▲
Cappadocia
The fairy-chimney landscape of Göreme — volcanic rock carved into churches, underground cities and cave dwellings, with the dawn balloon launches. Singular, and connects to your rock-cut/grotto thread. rock-cut arc
▲▲▲
Ephesus
The best-preserved classical city in the Mediterranean — the Library of Celsus, the great theatre, the marble streets. A Greco-Roman summit above even Pompeii for scale.
▲▲
Pamukkale + Hierapolis
The white travertine terraces with ruins on top; near-▲▲▲ for the singular landform.
▲▲
Göbekli Tepe / Mount Nemrut / the Lycian coast
Göbekli Tepe is the oldest monumental site on earth (~11,000 yrs) — near-▲▲▲ on significance, sparse to view; Nemrut's colossal heads and the turquoise gület coast round out a deep bench.
Jordan The Levant · stable
Generally stable and tourist-ready — the caveat is regional spillover; verify current conditions.
▲▲▲
Petra
The rose-red rock-cut Nabataean city — the Treasury, the Monastery, the Siq approach. One of the most singular sights on earth. Holds easily. rock-cut arc
▲▲
Wadi Rum + Jerash + the Dead Sea
The desert of Lawrence (a ▲▲ pairing with Petra), one of the best-preserved Roman provincial cities (Jerash), and the lowest point on earth. A strong supporting bench.
Israel & Palestine The Holy Land
Security-dependent and volatile — confirm conditions carefully before any planning.
▲▲▲
Jerusalem old city
The most layered sacred city on earth — the Western Wall, the Holy Sepulchre, the Dome of the Rock, all within one walled square kilometre. Incomparable; rated on merit, with the security asterisk.
▲▲
Masada / Bethlehem / the Galilee / Tel Aviv
The desert fortress, the pilgrimage sites, and Tel Aviv's Bauhaus-and-beach modernity; a deep ▲▲ bench when conditions allow.
Persia & Mesopotamia
Iran Persia
Politics, sanctions and visa situation are the live caveat — rated purely on heritage merit; feasibility shifts with the geopolitics.
▲▲▲
Isfahan
Naqsh-e Jahan Square — among the greatest architectural set-pieces on earth, ringed by the Shah and Sheikh Lotfollah mosques and the bazaar. A world-summit of Islamic architecture.
▲▲▲
Persepolis
The ceremonial capital of the Achaemenid empire — the great terrace, the apadana reliefs. A singular monument of the ancient Persian world.
▲▲
Shiraz / Yazd / the desert cities
Shiraz's gardens and poetry, Yazd's mud-brick old town and wind-towers, Persepolis's setting; a deep ▲▲ bench of Persian heritage.

The conflict frontier
Syria · Iraq · Lebanon · Yemen Rated on merit only
Active or recent conflict and serious travel warnings across much of this group — these are rated for completeness on heritage merit, NOT a recommendation to travel. Verify advisories; several are effectively off-limits.
▲▲▲
Palmyra (Syria) · Baalbek (Lebanon)
Palmyra's desert caravan-city ruins and Baalbek's colossal Roman temples are world-summit antiquity — rated on merit; access and preservation are the tragic, live problem.
▲▲
Damascus & Aleppo old cities · Ur / Babylon · Beirut
Among the oldest continuously inhabited cities on earth and the heart of Mesopotamian antiquity — extraordinary heritage, currently shadowed by conflict and reconstruction.

The honest framing: This region rivals Europe for heritage density — it is, after all, where cities, writing and several world religions began — but it is shadowed by safety and access more than anywhere else on these lists. The summits split into the stable-and-ready (Turkey, Jordan, the Gulf, Oman), the merit-with-a-political-asterisk (Iran's Isfahan & Persepolis), and the tragically-restricted (Jerusalem's volatility; Syria, Iraq, Lebanon, Yemen). A rating here is about the sight; the caveat is about whether you can safely stand in front of it.

The most "you" thread is unusually strong here: the rock-cut architecture arc reaches its western climax — Petra (Jordan), Cappadocia (Turkey), and the opening AlUla/Hegra (Saudi) extend the Longmen → Yungang → Mogao → Ellora → Lalibela line you've been tracing across Asia and Africa. Add Istanbul for the Byzantine thread and Persepolis/Isfahan for ancient Persia, and the most rewarding feasible trip right now is clearly Turkey (three ▲▲▲s, stable, hits three of your interests at once), with Jordan the natural pairing.

Safety and access in this region change faster than anywhere — every caveat here should be re-checked against current advisories before any planning.
