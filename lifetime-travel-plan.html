The master plan · a menu, not a syllabus

By window,
not by map

You won't visit them all — nobody does, and that's not failure. This sorts the world's summits by the one variable that actually forces the order: not geography, but when a thing has to be done — before a body changes, alongside someone whose window is narrowing faster, or whenever, because it'll keep.

The sorting logic. Three questions decide every entry. Does age close the door? (climbing, altitude, trekking, all-day walking in heat — these genuinely won't keep). Is it gentle and unchanging? (cities, cruises, museums — taxis and time fix everything, so they wait). Whose window is it? (your mum's narrows faster than yours, so moderate-effort shared trips move up — but never the extreme end). Everything below is ranked within each bucket by urgency and fit to how you actually travel.
Do now While the body is at its peak

The age-sensitive tier — climbing, altitude, multi-day treks, remote heat, all-day walking on uneven ground. None of these have a gentle version you can buy back later; they reward the mobility and energy you have at 21 and genuinely close with age. Do these in your strongest decade, around clinical breaks and any longer windows you can engineer.

1
Nepal — the Himalaya (Everest Base Camp / Annapurna)
The single most age-sensitive thing on any list. A two-week trek at altitude; there is no taxi version. The clearest "knees and lungs of your 20s–30s" trip. 2-week expedition
2
Patagonia — Torres del Paine + Fitz Roy
Multi-day trekking, far, and seasonal (their summer, Dec–Feb). World-summit wilderness that demands real walking. 2-week expedition
3
The Andean spine — Machu Picchu, Cusco, Titicaca, Uyuni
Altitude is the gatekeeper (3,000m+), and the Inca Trail can't be done gently. Strings the continent's deep-history and most surreal landscapes into one high-altitude run. 2-week expedition
4
The Silk Road grotto run — Dunhuang + a Datong–Pingyao loop
Remote NW desert, scorching, long days on your feet — and the supreme stretch of your own through-line. Physically taxing enough to want young legs. your art spine proper week+
5
Deep India — Hampi, Ellora & Ajanta, Rajasthan, Varanasi
Heat, extensive walking on uneven stone, guard-up logistics, and the rock-cut climax (Ellora). A demanding, seasoned-traveller's country — easier with youthful stamina. Best in winter (Oct–Mar). your art spine proper week+
6
Lhasa / Tibet — and the Tibetan-edge (Jiuzhaigou)
Altitude again — the body-tax that never eases. Your HK passport is a genuine edge here (no permit, travel freely). Needs a full week + acclimatisation. proper week
7
Bagan (Myanmar) — when safe
The temple plain rivals Angkor; extensive, hot, and access-dependent. On merit it's a now-trip; in practice, watch the security situation. proper week
8
Zhangjiajie + Huangshan
Cable cars defuse the ascent, but the clifftop walking and stairs are extensive — easier on young legs, and far better than fighting them at 60. proper week
9
Galápagos / Raja Ampat — the marine summits
Boat-based so not strictly age-gated, but snorkel/dive energy and the cost-of-effort favour doing them while fit and unencumbered. proper week+
With mum Her window is the one closing faster

The key realisation: her mobility narrows sooner than yours, so the moderate-effort shared trips move up the queue — do them while she's well able, not someday. But never the extreme end (no altitude, no trekking, no rough-country expeditions). Gentle, cultural, adaptable, and in the right season so weather is never the enemy. These echo the Florence / Venice / Paris trips she already loved.

1
Kyoto + Nara
The standout, and the direct successor to the European cultural trips. Supremely age-friendly (flat, taxis, benches, elective effort). Do it in autumn — comfortable, dry, autumn leaves; the perfect version. 5D4N
2
Penang (George Town) + Melaka
The gentlest genuinely-cultural option — flat, walkable UNESCO old towns, English everywhere, short flight, and the best food in SE Asia. Brief afternoon showers, worked around easily. 4–5D
3
Beijing — Mutianyu Wall + Forbidden City
The "use her moderate mobility now" trip: walking on the Wall is the thing that won't keep, and the cable car removes the brutal climb. Do it sooner, in autumn (dry, clear, no summer storms). 4D3N
4
Xi'an — the Terracotta Army
Moderate, low-strain sights, a real ▲▲▲, direct flight. Pairs with Luoyang's grottoes if you stretch it. Autumn for comfort. your art spine 4–5D
5
Guilin / Yangshuo — the Li River cruise
The boat does the work — one of the most restful ways to experience a ▲▲▲. Lovely with her; also genuinely bankable, so no rush, but a gentle joy whenever. 4–5D
6
Sri Lanka — the Cultural Triangle
Dambulla, Polonnaruwa, Anuradhapura — gentle heritage, with Sigiriya's climb optional (she does the water gardens, you climb). Warm; dry in the July rain-shadow. Adaptable to her pace. 5D+
7
European cities, together again
Rome, Vienna, Barcelona — the unticked heritage capitals, all gentle-with-taxis and squarely the register she loved. The natural continuation of the trips you've already shared. varies
8
Taipei — Palace Museum, food, hot springs
Not a ▲▲▲, but a supremely gentle, food-rich short break; the National Palace Museum is a world-class collection. Easy and warm. 4D3N
Bankable It'll keep — do it whenever a window opens

Gentle and unchanging: cities you walk at your own pace with taxis on tap, cruises where the boat does the work, museum-led trips where the reward is indoors and seated. Age barely touches these, so there's no urgency — they're the flexible filler for whatever window you have, and the trips to keep for the busier, less mobile, family-and-career decades when the expeditions get harder.

1
The great heritage cities
Rome, Istanbul, Barcelona, Tokyo, Mexico City, NYC, Prague. World-summit culture, all walkable-but-taxi-able. The closest thing to "do anytime, forever." (Turkey especially hits your Byzantine + classical + rock-cut threads at once.) Istanbul/Cappadocia: spine
2
Cruise-and-view scenery
Ha Long Bay (overnight), the Li River, the Yangtze Three Gorges. The vessel carries you; near-zero exertion. Genuinely better done slowly and later. short
3
Petra + Wadi Rum (Jordan)
Some walking but fully adaptable to pace; stable and tourist-ready. The western climax of your rock-cut arc — pairs with Cappadocia & AlUla. your art spine
4
The Great Barrier Reef + the Big Three Australia
Reef snorkelling is boat-based; Sydney and Uluru are gentle. The whole Australia "Big Three" is a dry-season (their winter) 2-week trip best saved and done properly, once. 2-week
5
Easter Island, the Anthropology Museum cities, the food capitals
Singular but low-strain — the seated, indoor, look-and-savour rewards. They wait happily.
6
Europe's last unticked summits
You've cleared most of the continent. What remains: Rome, the Acropolis, the Alhambra, Barcelona, the Swiss Alps, the Norwegian fjords, Iceland. The Alps/fjords/Iceland lean mildly active (do those a touch sooner); the rest are pure bankable. most of Europe: done

The honest arithmetic: of ~60–80 world ▲▲▲s, a committed lifetime of scarce travel windows might clear 40–60%. You will not get them all — and the list was never a checklist. It's a filter, so that when five days open in October or two weeks open between jobs, you reach for the thing that best fits that window and your wavelength, instead of defaulting to whatever's cheap.

You've already proved the method works: a single well-placed life-window — the London year — cleared most of a continent. You'll get more such windows (a fellowship, a sabbatical, a career break, retirement), and each can clear a cluster. The job now is sequencing: spend your strongest decade on the things that close (the treks, the altitude, the deep heritage walks), move the moderate shared trips up while your mum is well, and let the gentle, unchanging summits wait — because they always will.

The one through-line worth protecting across all three buckets: the eastward transmission of Buddhist and rock-cut monumental art — Longmen → Yungang → Mogao → Ellora & Ajanta → Bagan & Borobudur, reaching west into Cappadocia, Petra, and AlUla. If any single thematic spine organises the next few decades of your travel, it's that one — it's the deepest match to how you actually see.
