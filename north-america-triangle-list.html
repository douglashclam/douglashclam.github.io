North America
by the triangle scale

A continent that is landscape-rich and deep-built-heritage-thin — its summits are overwhelmingly natural, plus a handful of pre-Columbian sites and living cities. Three triangles withheld unless truly earned.

The honest framing: North America is landscape-rich and deep-heritage-thin next to Asia — there's no Angkor or Kyoto here because that built layer largely isn't there. The division of labour: Mexico carries the heritage weight (Mesoamerican sites + colonial cities + the best museum on the continent), the US carries the natural and living-city weight, and Canada is almost purely natural — one ▲▲▲ (the Rockies), with Québec City the lone serious heritage contender.

The most "you" thread: the US museum-and-geopolitics axis (DC + NYC's Met) and the Mexican pre-Columbian arc (Teotihuacán → Palenque → the Anthropology Museum) — the deep-history spine the continent does have.

Borderline calls left visible: NYC & Mexico City are ▲▲▲ on living-city totality (like Tokyo), not monuments; Utah is rated as a cluster; Chichén Itzá dropped to ▲▲ on the crowds-and-no-climbing reality. Live logistics — closures (e.g. Grand Canyon North Rim), wildfire, seasonal access — change often; verify before booking.
